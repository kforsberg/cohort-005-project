# Third-party notification services for Cadence

**Research date:** 2026-11-16  
**Scope:** Primary-source comparison for Wayfinder map [Cadence third-party notifications: build-ready product and technical spec](https://github.com/kforsberg/cohort-005-project/issues/4), research ticket [Research third-party notification services for Cadence](https://github.com/kforsberg/cohort-005-project/issues/8).

## Summary

Knock, Novu, and Courier are credible candidates for a combined persistent product inbox, email, and push. Each offers a backend API/workflow engine, user-level notification state, a customizable or prebuilt inbox, and channel/provider integrations. None removes the need for Cadence to decide who is allowed to receive each event, map Cadence identities and memberships, choose the push destination, define channel policy, or establish retention and deletion rules. This comparison does **not** choose a provider.

Prices and plan limits below are public list prices checked on the research date; plans and meters differ, so the figures are not directly equivalent. Delivery-provider charges (email/push) may also be separate.

### Pricing and operational/privacy notes

- **Knock:** Free includes up to 10,000 delivered messages/month and 500 guide recipients. Starter is $250/month for 50,000 messages, then $0.005/message. A message is one recipient on one channel, so inbox + email count twice; downstream channel providers bill separately. Public pricing lists 30-day logs for Free/Starter, 90 days for Enterprise, 99.9% uptime on self-serve plans, SOC 2 Type II/GDPR/CCPA, and HIPAA/BAA at Enterprise. Knock says identified user profiles are persisted; its docs say there is no mode that avoids storing user data. ([Pricing](https://knock.app/pricing), [Users](https://docs.knock.app/concepts/users))
- **Novu Cloud:** Free includes 10,000 workflow runs/month; Pro starts at $30/month for 30,000+ runs; Team starts at $250/month for 250,000+ runs. Published plan details include $1.20 per additional 1,000 runs on Pro/Team. Inbox retention is 30 days Free and 90 days Pro/Team; Enterprise offers custom retention. Security docs report SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR and HIPAA/BAA; US/EU residency is available on all plans, other listed regions are Enterprise. Open-source/self-hosted options exist; Cloud supports HMAC-authenticated Inbox sessions, important because otherwise a guessed subscriber ID and public application ID could expose another subscriber's feed. ([Pricing](https://www.novu.co/pricing/), [Security and compliance](https://docs.novu.co/platform/additional-resources/security), [Inbox production security](https://docs.novu.co/platform/inbox/prepare-for-production))
- **Courier:** Developer is free for 10,000 sends/month; Business is $0.005 per send; Enterprise has custom volume pricing. One template sent to one recipient counts once even when it uses multiple channels; test, failed and preference-blocked sends count, and external provider fees are separate. EU residency is available on paid plans in a separate Ireland datacenter; each region has separate data, workspaces and keys. Courier's Trust Center lists SOC 2 and GDPR materials; Enterprise pricing lists RBAC, observability integrations and an SLA. Inbox messages persist until archive/expiry, so Cadence would need an explicit expiry/retention policy. Profile deletion is available, but list subscriptions and preferences are separate resources and must also be removed as needed. ([Pricing](https://www.courier.com/pricing), [Billing](https://www.courier.com/docs/workspaces/billing.md), [Data centers](https://www.courier.com/docs/workspaces/datacenters.md), [Trust Center](https://security.courier.com/), [Profile deletion](https://www.courier.com/docs/api-reference/user-profiles/delete-a-profile.md))

Operational details worth validating in a proof of concept: message/provider retries and duplicate handling, rate limits/queue behavior, delivery and engagement event export, environment separation, secrets and client authentication, support/SLA scope, and what profile/feed deletion means for logs, backups and downstream providers. For example, Courier documents provider-specific send-rate limits that queue rather than drop excess sends; Novu can emit message webhooks and report inbox interactions; Knock exposes workflow/message logs and plan-dependent log retention. ([Courier rate limits](https://www.courier.com/docs/integrations/send-rate-limits.md), [Novu usage](https://docs.novu.co/platform/workflow/usage), [Knock pricing](https://knock.app/pricing))

## Cadence-specific implications and unknowns

The glossary makes a single exclusive `role` field unsafe as the sole audience key: a Team Admin is also a Student, and people can have course- or team-specific relationships. Keep Cadence as the authorization/source-of-truth system. Use a stable internal person ID with additive role/relationship data; derive recipients from the event's current course enrollment, instructor ownership, team/seat relationship, Q&A participation, or Platform Admin permissions. Vendor segments/topics can help fan out, but stale membership must not grant access or reveal restricted event content. ([Cadence glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md))

Questions still open for product/spec work:

1. **Push target and mechanism:** browser/web push, native mobile push, or both? The app surface, permission/opt-in journey, device-token lifecycle and expected delivery behavior are not specified.
2. **Event catalog and recipients:** which course, enrollment/purchase, team-seat, Q&A and admin events warrant a notification, and who qualifies at event time? How should multi-role recipients, actor exclusion and changing access be handled?
3. **State policy:** which of read/seen/opened/dismissed/archived matters; how long feed records live; whether expiry or deletion applies; and whether reading an email/push should update the in-app item.
4. **Channel/preferences:** first-release channels by event, mandatory vs optional communications, topic/channel opt-outs, unsubscribe behavior and transactional-email sender/provider ownership.
5. **Reliability and integration boundary:** trigger after commit or via an outbox; acceptable latency, retry/at-least-once semantics, idempotency keys, duplicate suppression, provider failure handling, and reconciliation for missed events.
6. **Privacy, operations and cost:** allowed user/event fields, data minimization, residency, DPA/subprocessor review, deletion propagation/backup retention, audit/log access, required SLA, expected volume, billing meter, and provider charges.

These questions require Cadence requirements and/or vendor trial/account review; public docs alone do not establish fit, contractual guarantees, deliverability, or exact costs at Cadence's volume.
